# T3 — Discoverability & Self-Teaching (zero-instruction public)

## Focus
How walk-up visitors with NO instructions learn a touchless gesture UI: making gestures
discoverable and teaching them in the moment.

## Sub-questions
1. **Interaction blindness / immediate usability:** why people don't realize a display is
   interactive; Müller et al. public-display research; the "honeypot effect"; call-to-action.
2. **Attract loops / attract modes:** cycling gallery, "step up" prompts, showing prior
   participants; effect on approach and engagement.
3. **Hand/body mirror & palm cursor:** showing the user's own hand/silhouette as a cursor
   to teach that motion is tracked (Kinect "you-are-the-controller" mirror, cursor mapping).
4. **Progress rings / radial fill / dwell feedback:** communicating "keep holding,"
   arming, and completion; loading-ring semantics; preventing premature release.
5. **Affordances & signifiers for gestures:** ghost hands, animated demonstrations, icons
   (open palm ✋), labels ("Hold to…", "Swipe to…"); onboarding overlays that fade.
6. Best practices for one-shot, high-throughput public/museum use.

## Evidence to find
- Müller, Walter, Vogel/Balakrishnan public-display interaction papers (HTML/abstracts).
- Kinect HIG discoverability/engagement; museum interactive-exhibit UX write-ups.
- Ultraleap/Leap design guides on onboarding and hand visualization; Nielsen Norman on
  signifiers/affordances; gestural onboarding case studies.
